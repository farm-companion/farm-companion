# Product

## Register

product

## Users

UK consumers hunting for genuine local food: people who want a real farm shop near them or near where they are travelling, want to know it is open right now, and want to know what is in season this month. They arrive mostly from organic search on a phone, often outdoors or in a car, with low patience and one job in mind. A second audience is farm owners who claim and maintain their listing, plus a small admin team who verify farms, moderate photos, and manage produce data.

## Product Purpose

Farm Companion is the UK's farm shop directory: 1,300+ verified farms found through an interactive map, real-time open and closed status, seasonal produce guides, and community photos. It exists to connect people with authentic local food and to make discovery fast and trustworthy. Success looks like a first-time visitor finding a relevant, open farm shop within seconds of landing, trusting that the listing is real, and coming back each season to see what is fresh.

## Brand Personality

Fresh, vibrant, seasonal, on a foundation of PuredgeOS clarity and verified-farm trust. The product should feel alive and produce-forward, with seasonal color and motion that shift through the year, while staying instantly legible and calm enough to use one-handed outdoors. Voice is clear, human, and confident: plain language, specific nouns and verbs, no marketing buzzwords, no hype. Warmth and energy come from accent color, real photography, and copy, never from clutter or novelty for its own sake.

## Anti-references

This product must not look like any of the following:

- Generic SaaS dashboard: cards everywhere, the big-number hero-metric template, identical icon-heading-text grids, corporate navy-and-teal. The default AI product look.
- Cluttered Yelp or aggregator directory: dense ad-heavy listings, banner clutter competing with content, a low-trust scraped-data feel.
- Twee rustic-farm cliche: chalkboard scripts, kraft-paper and barnwood textures, overly folksy badges, the obvious farm stereotype.
- Cold sterile minimalism: stripped so far it loses warmth and trust, gray-on-white, clinical, no personality.

Also banned by PuredgeOS regardless of surface: gratuitous motion that fights clarity, mystery-meat controls, and copy that blames the user.

## Design Principles

- Clarity is the covenant. If a first-time user cannot understand the screen and its primary action at a glance, it is not done. Clarity wins over decoration every time.
- Trust is the product. Verified status, real open and closed state, and real photography are the reasons people stay. Design must make trustworthiness visible, never imply more certainty than the data supports.
- Map-first discovery. The directory is a tool: finding, filtering, and comparing real places is the backbone, and the map is the primary way in. Marketing and editorial surfaces (home, /best top picks, /seasonal, /about) earn a brand register per task, but they serve discovery, not the other way around.
- Seasonality is a living rhythm. What is fresh this month is core content, not a gimmick. Seasonal shifts in color, imagery, and featured produce keep the product feeling current and worth revisiting.
- God-tier craft, outdoors-ready. Apple-level execution and instant performance, tuned for a phone in one hand in real light. Polish serves the task, not the portfolio.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA is the minimum across all surfaces, AAA where feasible, per PuredgeOS. Body text holds at least 4.5:1 contrast, interactive targets at least 48px, full keyboard operability with visible focus, logical focus order, and screen-reader support with proper landmarks and labels. Every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion alternative. The product is built to stay usable at 200% text zoom, in forced-colors and high-contrast modes, and on low-end devices on slow networks.
